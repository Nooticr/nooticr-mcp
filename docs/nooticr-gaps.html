Where we actually are

Worth stating plainly because it reframes the roadmap: the usual advice for a 36-tool MCP server is “add prompts, add resources, add a UI.” All three are already done. Spending the next quarter on protocol surface would be spending it on solved problems.

resources ✓

~71 registered. Two MCP Apps views per tool for 34 of 36 tools, in both Claude’s and ChatGPT’s widget mimes, plus a JSON watchlist resource.

prompts ✓

7 slash-command entry points — creator teardown, niche briefing, draft check, repurpose, and three more.

elicitation ✓

One call site: spend confirmation above 6 credits on the two variable-cost tools. Fails open by design.

completions ✓

On prompt arguments (platform, depth, country). Not on tool inputs.

tasks ✓

Experimental Tasks on the 3 slow tools, with a Durable Object-backed store so they survive eviction.

sampling ✗

Correctly absent. Unsupported by every mainstream client and on the deprecation path in SEP-2577.

logging ✗

Also correctly absent — same deprecation proposal. Nothing to do here.

annotations ✓

All 36 tools carry them — 31 readOnlyHint: true, 5 false. Already correct, and already the right hook for a publish tool’s confirmation.

output schemas ✓

Declared per tool, so clients get typed structured content rather than prose to re-parse.

Tier 0 Fix before adding anything

Small, cheap, and two of them are correctness problems rather than roadmap items.

We bill for fetches that cannot succeed

analyze_post, analyze_post_fast and understand_social_post each advertise nine-platform support and each unconditionally fan out to get_post_transcript — which the same repo documents as TikTok/YouTube-only. The call fails non-fatally, so nothing broke and nobody noticed. On seven of the nine advertised platforms, a credit is charged for a call that is structurally impossible. Gate the transcript leg on platform, or drop its cost from the quote.

The “ten networks” claim doesn’t survive the code

honesty

LinkedIn appears in only 2 of 13 network-taking tools (get_user_posts, analyze_creator_profile) and is absent from discovery, creator search, sounds, hashtags, mentions, transcripts, and the nine-platform lists the composite tools advertise. Separately, search_creators’ own description promises five platforms while its schema accepts three — a comment explains YouTube 400s upstream and Douyin returns hollow user objects, which is the right call, just not the documented one.

Either narrow the claim to “nine networks, plus LinkedIn creator reads,” or close the gap. Overclaiming in a listing that gets reviewed before publication is the expensive version of this bug.

Tier 1 Already built. Not exposed.

The backend has three pools of tool-shaped capability the public MCP server never registers. This is the highest value-per-hour work available, and none of it is new engineering.

Scheduled brand monitoring

built & unit-tested

The single most valuable unexposed thing we own. create_brand_watch, list_brand_watches and stop_brand_watch already exist server-side with recurring cadence, email digests, a budget cap and debit-first billing — complete with its own test suite.

This matters more than its cost suggests, because monitoring that runs without being asked is the entire premise of the social listening category, and today we have no scheduler anywhere: catch_up_watchlist computes its diff only when you call it, and the repo says so in a comment. We have the pull half of the feature shipped and the push half sitting unregistered. It also changes the revenue shape from per-query to recurring.

The blind spot Mentions we structurally cannot see

This is the strongest single item in the review, and it isn’t on any competitor’s feature list at accessible pricing. It also happens to sit on primitives we already own.

Text search systematically misses the mentions that matter most

search_mentions matches a term against captions, post bodies and comments. So a brand is found when someone types its name — and missed when someone says it. That bias isn’t random: the most damaging content is exactly the content least likely to name the brand in text. A three-minute rant, a “things I regret buying,” a side-by-side comparison — the name is spoken aloud, the caption says “watch till the end 😭”, and we return zero results.

A brand should be able to learn it was criticised in a video, and read the transcript of what was actually said. Nothing in the product does that today.

There are four ways a brand appears in short-form video. We detect one.

Spoken-word monitoring is nearly free on TikTok and YouTube

ship first

The architectural detail that makes this viable: get_post_transcript doesn’t run our own speech recognition — it reads the platform’s existing caption track, and returns available:false with a reason when a post has none. That is why it costs 1 credit and why it covers TikTok and YouTube only: those two auto-generate captions.

So the first version of spoken-mention monitoring is a matching layer over data we can already pull cheaply, on the two networks where short-form brand criticism actually happens. Coverage will be honestly incomplete — videos without a caption track are invisible — and that’s a disclosable limit, not a blocker.

Code src/shared/tools.ts:1100–1120 — “by reading its caption track… available:false when the post has no captions”

Everywhere else needs real speech recognition

genuine build

Instagram, X, Reddit and the four Chinese networks don’t hand us a caption track, so spoken monitoring there means running ASR over the audio ourselves — a real inference cost per minute of video, not a 1-credit read. Worth doing, worth pricing differently, and worth sequencing after the caption-track version proves the demand.

This also cleanly resolves the Tier 0 billing bug: the fix for “we charge for transcripts on platforms where they can’t work” and the foundation for this feature are the same piece of work — making transcript coverage explicit per platform instead of implicit.

Burned-in overlay text is a TikTok-shaped blind spot

OCR over existing frames

A creator types “don’t waste your money on this” as an on-screen overlay, never says it, never captions it. On TikTok that’s a native format convention, not an edge case — and it’s invisible to both text search and transcripts. We already extract scene-change-sampled frames; this is OCR over frames we can already fetch.

Visual presence, with the market’s own number attached

frames already return images

Talkwalker’s pitch for image recognition claims up to 80% of brand images carry no text mention at all — their argument for why text-only listening under-counts, and it applies with more force to video than to stills. Brandwatch sells the same capability as Image Insights, enterprise tier only.

Our version has an unusual advantage: get_post_frames already returns frames as real image blocks, so the client’s own model can see them. We don’t have to train a vision model to answer “is our product on screen here” — we have to route frames to a model that already can.

The funnel is the actual design problem

solved by an existing budget cap

You cannot know whether a video mentions a brand until you have transcribed it, so naive monitoring transcribes everything and the cost explodes — 100 credits spent to surface three hits. The feature is only viable as a funnel: narrow candidates on cheap signals first (watchlisted creators, niche plus keyword, hashtag, engagement threshold, caption near-miss), then transcribe the survivors, then send only the smallest set to frames.

Which is precisely what the unexposed create_brand_watch was already built with — cadence, an email digest, and a budget cap with debit-first billing. A modes: [text, spoken, onscreen, visual] parameter on top of that existing budget cap is the whole product shape: “watch for spoken mentions of our brand on TikTok and YouTube, up to 40 transcripts a day.”

Code crates/server/src/brand_watch.rs — budget cap and debit-first billing already implemented and tested

Why this outranks everything in Tier 2. Adding sentiment or share-of-voice makes us comparable to incumbents on their own axes. This makes us the only accessible product that can tell a brand what was said about it in a video — the crisis-detection use case, which carries the highest urgency and the highest willingness to pay in the whole category. Enterprise vendors gate audio and image recognition behind five-figure annual contracts; nothing found in the research does spoken-word brand monitoring on short-form video at self-serve pricing. And we are two primitives and a scheduler away, all three of which already exist.

Tier 2 Category table stakes we don’t have

Every one of these is standard in the products buyers compare us against, and every one is buildable from data we already fetch — no platform partnership, no gated API.

Sentiment, as a server-side tool

real build

Universal in the category and absent here. We return comments and platform-clustered themes; we never score them. The design worth copying is Facebook MCP’s filter_negative_comments: classify server-side and return the verdict, rather than dumping raw comments into context and asking the model to grade each one — cheaper in tokens and more consistent between runs.

One honesty note: reported real-world accuracy across the whole industry sits at 59–87% in aggregate and worse per-post, with sarcasm unsolved. Ship it as directional, and say so — Sprout gates sentiment behind a $399/seat tier and still draws accuracy complaints.

Share of voice and competitive leaderboards

real build

We have track_competitor, which scores one creator against their own median — genuinely good, and not what buyers mean by competitive benchmarking. Missing: percentage of category conversation held by you versus named rivals, and Rival IQ-style side-by-side leaderboards on engagement, post frequency and follower growth. Pure aggregation of public data we can already read.

Audience quality / fake-follower scoring

real build

The core of the influencer-vetting market — HypeAuditor models 53 behavioural signals, Modash checks 1,200 fraud factors — and it’s built entirely from follower-growth curves, engagement timing and comment quality. No special access required, just statistical modelling on data we fetch today.

This also closes a gap the product currently admits: who_should_i_work_with explicitly declines to measure audience overlap because it would cost ~9 credits per candidate. Honest, and a hole a competitor fills.

Alerting and spike detection

needs Tier 1 scheduler

Near-universal at mid-tier and up, and impossible for us today for one reason: nothing runs in the background. Falls out almost free once scheduled brand watch is exposed — the engine is the same, the addition is a threshold and a notification.

Trend lifecycle and virality forecasting

real build

We surface trending sounds and hashtags as snapshots. Exolyt tracks how a hashtag or sound trends over time and where it spreads; its Growth Prediction score forecasts follower trajectory; Dash Social predicts post performance before publish. All ML on engagement time series — well within reach, and it maps directly onto reads we already do.

Scheduled reporting and bulk export

real build

Unglamorous and a documented retention driver — agencies buy recurring, on-brand, client-facing reports as a separate SKU (AgencyAnalytics, DashThis, Determ all sell it that way). It also solves a real technical problem: large pulls should leave the context window entirely. Return a resource_link to a generated CSV/PDF, the way the best YouTube MCP hands back bulk report jobs, instead of inlining rows.

Tier 3 Where we could be alone in the market

Two of these need engineering. The first mostly needs saying out loud.

We own a moat we don’t market

already shipped

Enterprise vendors — Synthesio, Brandwatch, Meltwater, Talkwalker — charge $36–50K per year specifically for Chinese platform coverage, and essentially nothing below enterprise tier covers Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Weibo or Bilibili at all. We cover all four as baseline. Meltwater and Brandwatch only added TikTok listening in 2025; we treated it as first-class from day one.

Nothing found in the research combines this network list with both raw reads and AI-native content helpers in one product. That’s the positioning, and today the README leads with “ten networks” instead.

Natural-language monitoring setup

our natural advantage

The category’s most persistent onboarding complaint is hand-crafting Boolean queries; Brand24’s AI topic clustering exists specifically to remove that burden. We are an LLM-native product by construction — a user describes what to watch in a sentence and we compile the query. This is the one place where being an MCP server first is a structural advantage rather than a distribution choice.

Share of voice inside AI answer engines

cheap, novel

How often does ChatGPT or Perplexity name your brand versus a competitor when asked about your category? Brand24 shipped this in 2025 and it is still a differentiator rather than table stakes. Implementation is unglamorous — query the models, log and compare mentions over time — and it is a conspicuously apt thing for a company that lives on the MCP surface to measure.

Closing the loop to publish

highest value, highest risk

Every tool we have stops at the recommendation. The backend already holds enqueue_publish_job, schedule_post, compose_sequence, overlay_bake, spawn_variants and bake_job_status. Ayrshare is the closest competitor shape here — one surface, publish and schedule across 13+ platforms — and it prices per connected profile, which rhymes with our credit model.

Do it last and do it carefully. Publishing is irreversible, an LLM is the caller, and the mitigations are already patterned in our own codebase: destructiveHint so clients raise a confirmation, real per-call scope enforcement, and the two-step quote-then-confirm flow create_brand_watch already uses.

What not to build

Each of these is gated by something we cannot buy our way past at our size. Knowing them is worth as much as the roadmap.

Historical X archives, Instagram competitor data, LinkedIn at scale

platform-gated

Full-archive X search exists only on an enterprise tier reported at $42–50K/month; the self-serve path was discontinued and no bulk backfill is purchasable at any self-serve price. Instagram’s Graph API structurally returns data only for the connected account — competitor analytics and audience demographics for accounts you don’t own aren’t rate-limited, they’re absent, and Meta cut third-party limits from 5,000 to 200 requests/hour. LinkedIn’s API terms prohibit scraping outright, with real ban and civil exposure regardless of the hiQ precedent on public data.

Third-party audience demographics

inference, not data

Age, gender and location for someone else’s audience is inferred from bios and names, not sourced. Vendors are retreating from it on both accuracy and privacy grounds. Don’t ship a number we’d have to defend.

Journalist databases, PR distribution, enterprise vision models

different business

Meltwater’s media-contacts database is a licensing-and-CRM business, not an API-reads business. Brandwatch and Talkwalker’s logo-in-untagged-image detection needs purpose-built vision models at scale. Both are out of reach and neither is what our users came for.

Server-initiated sampling, and native async Tasks as a product bet

deprecated / unstable

Sampling is unsupported by every mainstream client and formally proposed for deprecation in SEP-2577 alongside Roots and Logging. Native Tasks are being restructured into the Extensions framework with a redesigned lifecycle. We already use experimental Tasks on three tools, which is fine — just don’t make either a load-bearing part of a new feature. Roll our own job pattern with plain tools; it works on every client today.

Two things to decide, not build

A stated position on public-data privacy. Sixteen privacy regulators have jointly affirmed that publicly-posted personal data remains subject to data-protection law — “it was public” is not a lawful basis under GDPR, and consent at listening scale is structurally impossible. The industry’s answer is data minimisation, pseudonymisation and a legitimate-interest argument. Every competitor is quiet about this. Having a written position is cheap, and it is the kind of thing an enterprise buyer asks about on the first call.

Whether transparent pricing is a feature. Roughly 72% of listening tools use custom enterprise packaging; the loudest complaint across G2, Trustpilot and Reddit for Sprout, Hootsuite and Meltwater is pricing — opacity, renewals that double, auto-renew traps. We meter per fetch, publish the number in every tool description, and charge nothing for the tools that don’t fetch. That is the opposite of the category norm and currently reads as an implementation detail rather than a stance.
